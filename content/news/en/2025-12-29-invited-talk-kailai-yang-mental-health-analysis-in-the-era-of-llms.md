On December 29, 2025, the **Language and Information Center lecture series** hosted **Dr. Kailai Yang**, who gave a talk titled **“Mental Health Analysis in the Era of Large Language Models”** to faculty and students of the School of Artificial Intelligence, Wuhan University.

Dr. Yang received his PhD in Computer Science from the University of Manchester, where his research focused on large language models. He has published more than 20 papers with over 1,700 citations, with lead-author work appearing at NeurIPS, ACL, EMNLP, WWW, IEEE TAC and Information Fusion. He is the lead author of **MentaLLaMA**, the first open-source series of instruction-following, interpretable large language models for mental health analysis, and serves as an Area Chair of LREC 2026.

Mental health problems are an increasingly urgent threat to global public health, yet existing analysis methods generalize poorly across tasks and offer little interpretability in their diagnoses. The talk examined how large language models can address these limitations along three lines: integrating emotion-related signals from affective computing into mental-health-oriented LLMs; using LLMs to evaluate and improve the generalization and interpretability of mental health analysis; and developing advanced post-training techniques that further enhance LLM performance in both mental health and general domains. Together, these results show that LLMs combined with affective computing and targeted post-training can substantially improve the quality, transparency and accessibility of automated mental health analysis systems.

The talk was followed by an extended discussion with lab members. We thank Dr. Yang for the visit.
