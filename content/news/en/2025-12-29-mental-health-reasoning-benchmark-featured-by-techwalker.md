On December 29, 2025, **TechWalker** published a feature titled *“Wuhan University launches a mental health AI assistant: a language model that thinks and judges like a professional counsellor”*, reporting on our lab's latest work on reliable mental health reasoning with large language models.

The work, released on arXiv as **MiraMind: Benchmarking Reliable Mental Health Reasoning beyond Answer Accuracy** (arXiv:2512.09636), is led by PhD student **Mengxi Xiao** under the supervision of **Prof. Qianqian Xie** and **Prof. Min Peng**, in collaboration with **Prof. Sophia Ananiadou** at the University of Manchester and colleagues from partner institutions. On December 16, 2025, the paper was featured as the **#1 Paper of the Day** on Hugging Face Daily Papers.

![#1 Paper of the Day on Hugging Face](./assets/news-20251216-huggingface-paper-of-the-day.png)

The work makes two contributions:

- **A benchmark for reasoning quality.** Beyond answer accuracy, the benchmark assesses whether a model's reasoning about cognitive assessment, diagnosis and intervention strategy is systematic and evidence-constrained. The evaluation reveals a “restraint gap”: models often express unwarranted certainty given limited evidence.
- **Mindora, a post-trained model.** Using a hybrid supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning recipe, the 8B-parameter Mindora improves evidence-constrained reasoning and ranked best among 20 compared models.
