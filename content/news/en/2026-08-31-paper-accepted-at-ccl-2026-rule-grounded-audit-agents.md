The **25th China National Conference on Computational Linguistics (CCL 2026)** took place in Yichang, Hubei, on **15–18 October 2026**. Organized by the Chinese Information Processing Society, the conference featured invited talks, thematic forums, paper presentations, shared tasks and system demonstrations. Faculty and students from the Cognitive Language and Information Lab (CLAIN) contributed to conference organization, volunteering and research exchange, with a collaborative paper accepted to the conference programme.

For the lab, the event offered opportunities to share research, learn about developments in the field and participate in the work that supports an academic conference. Faculty and students took part in these activities in several different capacities.

## Min Peng Serves as Local Chair and Hosts the Opening Ceremony

**Prof. Min Peng served as Local Chair of CCL 2026 and hosted the opening ceremony.** Local organization connects conference preparations with activities on site, requiring coordination of arrangements and support for academic exchange. Prof. Peng contributed to the coordination of the local organizing work.

The main conference opened on 17 October. Prof. Peng hosted the opening programme, which was followed by invited talks, oral presentations and poster sessions covering computational linguistics and artificial intelligence.

## Qianqian Xie Hosts the AI4Humanity Forum and Closing Ceremony

**Prof. Qianqian Xie served as Poster Chair and hosted the AI4Humanity forum.** Topics included value alignment, low-resource language processing, trustworthy agents, intelligent decision-making and healthcare, linking advances in AI with human needs and social applications.

These questions connect with CLAIN's research on language understanding, trustworthy evaluation, medical AI and financial intelligence. The forum provided a setting to discuss how technical capabilities can translate into reliable support in real-world applications.

**Prof. Xie also hosted the closing ceremony and announced the conference's highlight paper, poster and demonstration awards.** These roles formed part of the lab's contribution to the academic organization of CCL 2026.

## Research on Rule-Grounded Audit Agents

The collaborative paper **“Seeing Is Not Knowing: A Benchmark for Rule-Grounded Audit Agents,”** first-authored by **Maowei Jiang**, was accepted to the English-language poster programme. The work focuses on evaluating audit agents that must use explicit rules when interpreting observed information.

Auditing requires connecting evidence with applicable rules: identifying relevant information, determining whether a rule applies and supporting a judgment with an appropriate basis. These tasks place demands on an agent's information understanding, rule use and reasoning.

The research introduces a benchmark around this problem, bringing rule grounding into the evaluation of audit agents. This direction connects with the lab's interest in reliable reasoning, verifiable judgments and evaluation grounded in practical tasks.

**Authors (in paper order):** Maowei Jiang, Jingchi Yang, Guiqi Hua, Ziyan Kuang, Jie Gong, Ziyang Xu, Zhiwei Liu, Jiajia Huang, Chao Xu, Min Peng, Qianqian Xie.
