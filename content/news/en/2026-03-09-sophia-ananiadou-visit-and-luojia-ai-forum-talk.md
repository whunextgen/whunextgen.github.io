From **March 7 to 11, 2026**, **Prof. Sophia Ananiadou** visited the Cognitive Language and Information Lab at the School of Artificial Intelligence, Wuhan University.

Prof. Ananiadou is Professor of Computer Science at the University of Manchester, Director of the National Centre for Text Mining (NaCTeM), Deputy Director of the Institute for Data Science and Artificial Intelligence, Honorary Professor at the University of the Aegean, a member of the Greek Ministry of Research and Innovation's AI expert group, and an Adjunct Professor at the School of Artificial Intelligence, Wuhan University. She is an ELLIS Fellow and Turing Fellow, has published more than 400 papers in venues including Nature Human Behaviour and NeurIPS, and with over 26,000 citations is among the twenty most-cited scholars in text mining worldwide.

## Luojia AI Forum, 22nd session

On the morning of **March 9**, Prof. Ananiadou gave the 22nd lecture of the **Luojia AI Forum** in Room A237, titled **“What 20 Years of Text Mining Taught Us About AI: Lessons from the National Centre for Text Mining”**.

![Prof. Ananiadou lecturing](./assets/news-20260309-ananiadou-lecture-1.jpg) ![Systematic review automation](./assets/news-20260309-ananiadou-lecture-2.jpg)

The talk distilled two decades of research and deployment into four lessons about AI. First, language encodes processes, not just words: understanding events and mechanisms is essential for modelling scientific knowledge. Second, intelligence requires synthesis across documents, as demonstrated by systematic review automation and large-scale summarisation. Third, models do not operate in isolation but within scientific, clinical and analytical workflows, so language technologies must align with how experts think and act. Finally, the era of large language models does not remove the need for structure and domain adaptation; culturally aware and financial LLMs show how intelligence must be engineered for context. Rather than a retrospective, the lecture used this experience to set out a forward-looking vision for knowledge-aware AI systems.
